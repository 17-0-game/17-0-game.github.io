The seven-slot Sleeper challenge

What is the 17-0 game?

The Sleeper 17-0 game is a seven-pick NFL fantasy football challenge. Each roll gives you a franchise and a season, then you draft one eligible player from that pool. Fill the lineup and the game awards a projected record. Seventeen wins and no losses is the target.

The catch is the season. You might recognize a future Hall of Famer and still be looking at an ordinary fantasy campaign. Read the card’s PPR fantasy points per game (FPPG), then check the chemistry it creates with players already on your roster. The name alone is not the pick.

New to the board? The first-draft walkthrough covers the controls. Below, a finished lineup shows exactly where one real score came from.

Know which jobs remain

📋Seven roster slots, with one flexible opening

You need one quarterback, two running backs, two wide receivers, one tight end and one FLEX. FLEX accepts a running back, receiver or tight end. It cannot replace a missing dedicated QB, RB, WR or TE slot.

If you already have a running back, another is still required. If you have a tight end and an empty FLEX, a second tight end can be eligible. The same card can therefore be useful early and unusable later, depending on what you have filled.

In the wide layout, a strong tight end can go into TE while FLEX stays available for three position types. That is a placement decision to make before confirming. Use the roster eligibility guide for the full map and the FLEX guide for the trade-offs.

A connection applies to both players in the pair. One +2 link therefore adds four team chemistry points when fully counted. Elite and Legendary labels identify designated special duos; a famous partnership does not earn one just because you expect it to. Check the actual breakdown in the chemistry guide.

The projected record is the result of this fantasy challenge. It is not proof that seven players would beat a real NFL schedule. Use the scoring reference to read the labels, then use the completed draft below to follow an actual total.

Where the extra ten points came from

Barkley and Jones received a Same Team connection worth +2 to each player: four team points. Barkley–Andrews, Barkley–Chubb and Andrews–Chubb each received a +1 Same Draft connection to both players: two team points per pair.

That is 4 + 2 + 2 + 2 = 10 chemistry. Add it to the 107.2 base and the displayed total becomes 117.2. The chemistry reference explains how to read the other recognized connection types.

🧠

What this result helps you review

The links improved this roster’s total, but they did not turn it into a perfect-record lineup. That is a useful distinction when a colorful connection makes a modest player season look irresistible.

A finished list also leaves out the alternatives offered during the draft. It cannot prove which earlier selection should have changed. Record the candidate you passed on while the board is still visible, then use the pick comparison tool to compare the actual point gains.

Two different resources

🔄Spend the reroll that changes the right thing

The two rerolls solve different problems. Each is available once per draft, so first identify which part of the offer you want to preserve.

🎰 Team reroll

Changes the franchise and keeps the season. Use it when that season still interests you but the current franchise’s usable players offer too little for your open jobs.

📅 Season reroll

Changes the season and keeps the franchise. The button is labeled Year in the game. Use it when you want another season from the team already on the board.

A reroll is not a request for a particular player. Before spending it, name a usable current option and the kind of improvement you are looking for. The reroll decision guide works through the harder early- and late-draft cases.

💬17-0 game questions

Can you go 17-0 in this game?

Yes. The 17-0 game can award an unbeaten projected record when the completed FPPG-plus-chemistry total is high enough. That is the target, but a famous lineup or a few strong links do not guarantee it. The perfect-record guide explains what you can control during a draft.

Can a quarterback go in FLEX?

No. FLEX accepts a running back, wide receiver or tight end. You have one dedicated QB opening. A second quarterback cannot fill another position, regardless of his FPPG. The position map shows every legal destination.

Can I move a selected player to another slot?

Some roster layouts provide movement between eligible slots. A filled field card may instead highlight chemistry when selected. Use the control offered by your screen and choose the initial destination carefully. Moving an existing player is separate from replacing him or reopening an earlier pool; the rules guide covers that distinction.

Do I need to connect a Sleeper username?

The username field is optional for the basic draft. You can read the board, choose players and view the completed result without connecting one.

Why can’t I start another run?

The game has its own remaining-plays meter. If the allowance is used up, follow the waiting information shown in the game. Reloading the play window does not add another play.

What should I do if the game window is blank?

Press Play once and allow the game to load. Try Theater for more room. If it remains blank, the 17-0 loading checklist helps separate a connection problem from a browser setting.
